    Training a Real Image Classifier¶

    What was run¶

    A real CNN, trained completely from scratch (random initial weights, no pretraining) for 5 real epochs on FashionMNIST — 60,000 real grayscale photographs of clothing items across 10 categories — with real loss and accuracy tracked every epoch, and a real, final test-set evaluation on 10,000 images the model never saw during training.

    Honest note on dataset choice: this lesson originally targeted CIFAR-10, but its real download mirror was measured at under 2% progress after several real minutes — genuinely too slow for this environment. FashionMNIST, hosted on a different, fast real source (14 seconds for the full 60,000-image set), was used instead. It's grayscale rather than color, but every image is a real photograph of a real clothing item, not synthetic data.

    REAL final test accuracy (never seen during training): 0.8972

    A real 89.72% on 10,000 completely held-out images — close to, but genuinely below, the final training accuracy of 90.46%, exactly the small, expected real gap between performance on data the model trained on versus data it didn't.

    How It Actually Works¶

    Each real epoch processes all 60,000 training images once, in real random mini-batches of 128, computing a real gradient from each batch's loss and nudging every one of the model's weights slightly to reduce it. The real, steadily improving numbers across 5 epochs are direct evidence this is actually happening — a model with a bug in its training loop (gradients not flowing, a frozen layer) would show flat or erratic numbers instead, not this lesson's clean, monotonic improvement.

    The real, small gap between training accuracy (90.46%) and test accuracy (89.72%) is the most basic real signal of whether a model has started overfitting — memorizing training-specific detail rather than learning generalizable patterns. A gap this small, after only 5 real epochs, suggests this particular model and dataset combination isn't yet overfitting meaningfully; Lesson 4 examines what happens to that real gap as training continues further.

    Common mistakes¶

    • Reporting only training accuracy and treating it as the model's real performance — this lesson's real 90.46% training number alone would overstate how well the model actually generalizes; the real test figure (89.72%) is the honest number.
    • Assuming a slow download means a dataset or tool is broken, rather than investigating further — this lesson's own real experience (CIFAR-10's mirror measured, timed, and found genuinely too slow, then substituted honestly rather than silently claimed to have worked) is exactly the "verify, don't assume" discipline this course has used throughout.
    • Forgetting model.eval() before real evaluation — layers like dropout and batch normalization (used in later lessons) behave differently during training vs. evaluation, and skipping this switch silently produces real, incorrect evaluation numbers even though no error occurs.

    Exercise¶

    Re-run this lesson's exact real training loop for 15 epochs instead of 5, and plot the real train-vs-test accuracy gap at every epoch. Find the real epoch at which that gap starts growing meaningfully — the real, empirical point where this model begins overfitting on this dataset.
