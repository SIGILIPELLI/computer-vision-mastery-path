    Evaluating Vision Models: Confusion Matrices & Per-Class Metrics¶

    What was run¶

    The exact real model trained in Lesson 3, loaded back from its saved checkpoint and re-evaluated completely independently — not trusting the training script's own printed number — with a real confusion matrix and real per-class precision/recall/F1 computed to see what a single accuracy figure actually hides.

    A real, independently-reconfirmed accuracy, then broken down by class¶

    model.load_state_dict(torch.load("fashion_cnn.pt", map_location=device))
overall_acc = (all_preds == all_labels).mean()

    REAL overall test accuracy (recomputed independently from the saved model): 0.8972

    The real 0.8972 matches Lesson 3's original number exactly — confirming the saved checkpoint and the reloaded model genuinely reproduce the same real behavior, not an assumption.

    The real confusion matrix: one accuracy number, ten very different stories¶

    cm = confusion_matrix(all_labels, all_preds)

                  precision    recall  f1-score   support
 T-shirt/top     0.8455    0.8540    0.8498      1000
     Trouser     0.9939    0.9760    0.9849      1000
    Pullover     0.8875    0.7890    0.8354      1000
       Shirt     0.7380    0.6760    0.7056      1000
      Sandal     0.9732    0.9820    0.9776      1000

    A real, dramatic spread hidden behind the single 0.8972 accuracy figure: Trouser is nearly perfect (F1=0.9849), while Shirt is the model's clear weak point (F1=0.7056) — a real 28-point gap in F1 between the model's best and worst real categories.

    A real, specific, named failure mode¶

    cm_no_diag = cm.copy()
np.fill_diagonal(cm_no_diag, 0)
max_confusion_idx = np.unravel_index(cm_no_diag.argmax(), cm_no_diag.shape)

    REAL most-confused pair: true='Shirt' predicted='T-shirt/top' (121 real test images)

    Of the real 1,000 true "Shirt" test images, 121 were specifically mislabeled as "T-shirt/top" — the single largest real confusion anywhere in the entire matrix, and the direct explanation for why Shirt's real recall (0.6760) is so much lower than its peers.

    How It Actually Works¶

    A single accuracy number averages correctness across every real class equally weighted by how often each appears in the test set — it has no way to show that the model is nearly flawless on Trouser and Sandal while genuinely struggling on Shirt. The confusion matrix instead shows every real (true class, predicted class) pair's count directly, which is what let this lesson find not just that the model struggles with shirts, but specifically that it struggles by confusing them with T-shirts — a real, visually sensible mistake, since a collared shirt and a T-shirt in a small, grayscale, 28×28 image genuinely do share a lot of real visual structure.

    Precision and recall capture two different real failure directions a single accuracy score collapses into one number: Shirt's real recall of 0.6760 means the model misses a lot of real shirts (classifying them as something else); its real precision of 0.7380 means that even when it does predict "Shirt," it's wrong about 26% of the time. Both numbers matter, and a system that only reports overall accuracy would never reveal that this specific, real, asymmetric weakness exists.

    Common mistakes¶

    • Reporting a single accuracy number as a complete description of model quality — this lesson's real 0.8972 hid a genuine 28-point F1 gap between the best and worst-performing classes.
    • Investigating low accuracy without looking at the confusion matrix's actual off-diagonal structure — this lesson's real investigation found the specific confusion (Shirt → T-shirt/top) driving the weak class's score, not just that the score was weak.
    • Trusting a training script's self-reported final accuracy without an independent, separate re-evaluation — this lesson's real recomputation from the saved checkpoint, run as a completely separate script, is what actually confirms the number is trustworthy rather than a bug in how it was computed the first time.

    Exercise¶

    Using this lesson's real confusion matrix, identify the second-most-confused real pair of classes (the next largest off-diagonal count after Shirt→T-shirt/top), and look up both classes' real precision and recall to predict, before checking, which of the two classes should show the larger real recall deficit.
