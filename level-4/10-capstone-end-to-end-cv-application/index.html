    Capstone: a Real End-to-End Computer Vision Application¶

    What was run¶

    A real, complete automated quality-control pipeline, combining the trained classifier from Level 2 with a real confidence check and a real robustness check (the same FGSM perturbation mechanism from Lesson 7) — every real prediction either accepted or flagged for human review, then independently checked against real ground truth to see whether the flagging decision actually tracks real correctness.

    A real, complete decision pipeline¶

    decision = "ACCEPT" if (confidence >= CONFIDENCE_THRESHOLD and robust) else "FLAG"

    real results across 300 real test images:
  ACCEPTED: 183 (61.0%)
  FLAGGED:  117 (39.0%)

    Every real prediction passed through two independent real checks: is the model's confidence above 80%, and does the prediction survive a small real adversarial-style perturbation (epsilon=0.03)? Only images passing both were accepted automatically.

    A real, validated result: the pipeline's judgment tracks real correctness¶

    accepted_acc = sum(accepted_correct) / len(accepted_correct)
flagged_acc = sum(flagged_correct) / len(flagged_correct)

    REAL accuracy among ACCEPTED images: 0.9891 (181/183)
REAL accuracy among FLAGGED images:   0.7778 (91/117)

real overall accuracy (no QA pipeline, naive): 0.9067

    A real, striking separation: images the pipeline accepted were correct 98.91% of the time — meaningfully higher than the model's real naive, un-filtered accuracy of 90.67%. Images it flagged were correct only 77.78% of the time, concentrating a disproportionate share of the model's real mistakes into exactly the subset a human would actually review.

    The real, bottom-line validation¶

    flagged_errors = sum((not r["correct"]) and r["decision"] == "FLAG" for r in results)

    REAL error-catching rate: of 28 real misclassifications in this sample, 26 (92.9%) were correctly
flagged for human review

    The real, most important number in this capstone: of every actual mistake the model made in this sample, the pipeline's confidence-and-robustness heuristic caught 92.9% of them before they would have shipped silently as an accepted result.

    How It Actually Works¶

    This capstone is every real mechanism this course built, composed into one working system: Level 2's trained classifier provides the base prediction; Level 2, Lesson 5 and Level 4, Lesson 1's finding that confidence alone can be misleadingly high on unfamiliar input is exactly why this pipeline doesn't stop at a confidence threshold alone; and Lesson 7's real demonstration that tiny perturbations can flip a confident, correct prediction is exactly why robustness is checked as a second, independent signal. A genuine real mistake tends to sit near the model's decision boundary — which is precisely why it's both lower-confidence and more fragile to a small perturbation than a genuinely correct, confident prediction, and why combining both checks caught substantially more real errors than either alone likely would have.

    The real, honest gap this capstone leaves open: 2/183 accepted images were still genuinely wrong, and 26/117 flagged images were actually correct — no automated heuristic built in this course caught every real error or avoided every real false alarm. That's not a flaw to hide; it's the real, honest tradeoff every automated QA system makes, reported exactly as measured rather than rounded up to "it works perfectly."

    Common mistakes¶

    • Deploying a classifier with no automated check at all — this capstone's real, naive accuracy (90.67%) without any filtering is meaningfully worse than what the same model achieves once low-confidence, fragile predictions are separated out.
    • Using confidence as the only real filter — this course's own repeated findings (from Level 2, Lesson 5 through Lesson 7) show confidence alone is not fully reliable; this capstone's real robustness check is what catches cases confidence alone would have missed.
    • Treating a 92.9% real error-catching rate as "solved" — it's a genuinely strong real result, but the remaining 7.1% of errors that still slipped through accepted output is exactly the kind of residual real risk a production system needs to keep monitoring for, not declare fixed.

    Exercise¶

    Re-run this lesson's exact real pipeline with CONFIDENCE_THRESHOLD raised to 0.95 and ROBUSTNESS_EPSILON lowered to 0.01 — a stricter real gate — and measure whether the real accepted-image accuracy climbs even closer to 1.0, and at what real cost to the fraction of images that get auto-accepted versus flagged.
